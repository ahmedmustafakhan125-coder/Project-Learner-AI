When a learner asks "should I use X or Y", the useful answer is rarely a winner.
It is the axis the choice actually turns on.

## Name the axis first

Most tool comparisons reduce to two or three properties that genuinely differ,
surrounded by a lot that does not. Say what those are before comparing anything:
"these differ on how much they do for you, and on how much you can change when
they get it wrong."

## Give the condition, not the verdict

Replace "X is better" with the condition under which each wins:

- X, when the team is small and the schema is still moving.
- Y, when the schema is stable and there are many readers.

A learner who can state the condition can make the next decision alone. A learner
handed a verdict has to come back.

## Say when it does not matter

Plenty of choices are genuinely close. Saying "either is fine here, and the cost
of switching later is one afternoon" is more honest than manufacturing a
difference, and it teaches that reversibility is itself a criterion.

## Do not hide the cost of the recommended path

Every tool has a bill that arrives later — operational burden, lock-in, a
concept the learner must now hold. Name it at the moment of recommending, not
when it lands.
